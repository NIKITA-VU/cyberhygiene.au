We learned to wash our hands. Cyber hygiene is next.

We knew how to wash our hands long before 2020. It took a crisis to turn it into a habit, with posters in every hallway. Our digital lives deserve the same everyday care, without waiting for a crisis. Phones, watches, smart devices and AI chatbots are part of daily life, and so are the risks that come with them.

Know it

Simple, jargon-free skills: spot a scam, secure an account, check what an AI tells you.

Practise it

Hands-on activities, so the skill is in your fingers and not just in your head.

Make it a habit

Take-home checklists and small routines that stick, like washing your hands.

Why now?

Our lives moved online faster than our habits did. The advice is out there, but it hasn't reached most people in a form that sticks. Here is what the latest Australian figures show.

Every 6 minutes

On average, a cybercrime is reported in Australia (2024-25).

$33,000

The average self-reported cost of cybercrime for individuals (2024-25), up 8%.

$2 billion+

Reported scam losses by Australians in 2024.

Mostly preventable

Government cyber experts say basic habits can prevent the majority of reported incidents.

Sources: ASD's Australian Cyber Security Centre, Annual Cyber Threat Report 2024-25 (cyber.gov.au); National Anti-Scam Centre (scamwatch.gov.au).

What your group will do

About 30% explaining and 70% doing. Every idea comes with something to try within a couple of minutes.

Real or Fake?

Vote on real and fake messages, voice clips and images, then reveal the giveaways together.

Would You Paste It?

Green, amber or red: which information is safe to give an AI chatbot? The debate does the teaching.

Catch the AI's mistake

Teams quiz a chatbot on something they know well and hunt for errors and invented facts.

Five-minute privacy check

Everyone fixes two settings on their own phone, so they leave with something already done.
